## Snapshot

I'm late 40s and do shift work. I've never been to therapy and I've never filled in one of these thought record things. A friend gave me the book. I read it on my phone in two goes. The first was at the kitchen table after a late shift, about an hour and a half, as far as Chapter 04. The second was on my day off, about the same again, and took me to the end. I finished it. I nearly stopped at "Who This Is For" in the Introduction, because it told me straight out that the book is for people who've "read the books". I nearly stopped again at Chapter 04, which is all about "projects" and "shipping" and has nothing in it from my life. I didn't do the thought record. I did write my three things for a bad day on the back of an envelope.

Note for whoever collects these: my brief doesn't say which country I live in. So in question 3 I've judged the crisis section as someone who has to find her own number in that list, not as someone from one particular country.

## Verdict

There's a good small book in here trying to get out. The bits about doing five minutes, not waiting to feel like it, and having three small things for a bad day are things I could actually use tomorrow. But most of the book is written for someone who sits at a laptop, builds apps, has "read the books" and knows the words. I kept running into words I didn't know (psychoeducation, declarative, CBT, rumination, minimum viable, ship, portfolio) and examples that aren't my life (landing pages, frameworks, launches). It made me feel like I'd picked up somebody else's post. The crisis page at the front is clear and I'm glad it's there first. But I can't check whether the numbers are right, and the heaviest chapter (09) sends me back to the front for them instead of printing them where I am.

## What worked

- **Read This First.** It comes first, it's short, and "I'm not safe tonight and I need you with me" is something I could actually say. "It doesn't need to be explained well" was a relief to read.
- **Triage, "Find Your Chapter".** The left-hand column is in plain words: "The days are flat and empty", "I'm going round in circles in my head". That's the only place where rumination is explained in words I'd use.
- **Introduction, "Three Operating Rules", rule 3:** "Five minutes counts. A badly done exercise beats a skipped one." That made me feel I could have a go.
- **Introduction, rule 2:** the option to use voice memos. I can do that on a break. I can't sit and write.
- **Chapter 00, "Check the Body First",** where it mentions menopause. That's my age, and nobody puts it in these books.
- **Chapter 02, Stage 1:** "If hourly won't happen, three check-ins a day is fine. A rough log you keep beats a perfect one you abandon." That's the first bit of Chapter 02 that fit a working day.
- **Chapter 02, the grading table:** "Open one envelope", "Clear one surface", "Reply to one message". Those are my size.
- **Chapter 03, Worked Example Three (dishes, post, laundry).** That's the only one of the four that looked like my kitchen.
- **Chapter 05, "The Five-Minute Rule":** "genuine permission to stop". I believed it.
- **Chapter 08, the whole chapter,** especially the field sheet: wash face, one meal, two minutes of daylight. And "Never miss twice." I understood every word of it.
- **Chapter 10:** "spot it at the door rather than in the living room". Plain, and I got it straight away.

## Answers to the four targeted questions

**1. Do the four worked examples in Chapter 03 feel broad enough to include you?**
One of them does, Example Three, the dishes and post and laundry. Even then, "a team restructuring" sounds like an office. Example Four is close, because unopened letters are real life. But it has the money sitting in the account, and for a lot of people that's not true. That's exactly the time you'd need the example. Example Two, job applications, could be me if I'd been let go, so that one half fits. Example One is someone else's life entirely: apps, features "deployed", a "brand document", a degree. None of the four has kids, looking after a parent, a body that hurts, a boss, shifts, or being my age. I read Example One first, since it's labelled the author's own, and it set the tone that this chapter wasn't about me.

**2. Are the stop rules clear, and did you actually feel able to stop?**
The stop lines at the end of every chapter are clear and kind. "Stop here if stopping is what's available" is nice. The five-minute rule's "genuine permission to stop" is the clearest of all, and I believed it. The "stop reading" on the Read This First page is clear too. The unclear ones are in the Chapter 00 table, where three rows just say "Escalate" and don't say to whom. The drinking row especially needs to say who to go to. As for whether I felt able to stop: honestly, no, and not because the book pushed me. The Introduction tells me that reading end to end is "the failure", and then every chapter ending tells me to close the book. I'd been given it to read, so I kept going and felt told off at every chapter ending. The ending of Chapter 07 ("the chapter demonstrating itself") was the worst for that. The stop lines tell me to stop but not how to come back, like which bit to open next time.

**3. Are the crisis resources accurate and usable for your region?**
I can't check any of them from the book, so I'm flagging all of them as not checked by me. That covers 911, 999, 112, 000, the UAE 999 and 998, 988 "call or text", Samaritans 116 123 for the UK and Ireland, Lifeline 13 11 14, and findahelpline.com. The book says they were "current at the time of publication" but doesn't give a date, so I can't tell how old they are. It doesn't say whether the lines are free, whether they're open all night, or whether texting works in both the US and Canada or only one. What's missing, and which I can see without checking anything: India and the UAE get an emergency number but no one to just *talk* to. Everywhere not named, including the rest of Europe, gets "findahelpline.com", which is a website. In a bad moment, on a phone at 3am, that's a search, not a number. As for being usable: the list is only on the front page. It isn't repeated in Chapter 09, in Appendix B's quick reference, or on the Safety Plan, which asks me to write in a crisis line without telling me which one. Please have someone check every number against the services' own information before this is printed. I'm not the person who can say they're right.

**4. Does any chapter push too far without enough support, or leave you alone with something heavy?**
Chapter 09 does. It tells me my self-hate is something I "do because it works". I get that they're trying to say it isn't my fault, but it's a lot to take in. Then the table tells me it "costs the years the protection was supposed to save", and at nearly 50 that line hurts. The chapter ends with "Put it down. It will still be true tomorrow", and there's nothing about what to do if it's hit you. The line that it's worth asking a professional about if it "hit hard" is in Appendix D, and the numbers are at the front. Next is Chapter 03's "two months behind on rent" line. That's the one real money problem in the book, and it gets one sentence and no next step, after an example where the money was fine all along. Then Appendix C4's "One reason worth staying for", asked cold. Then Chapter 05, which tells me chest pressure is avoidance and to sit with it, without saying to get it checked if I'm not sure. Chapter 04's bit about approval being "scarce" when I was young opens a door. It closes it again quickly ("you don't need an origin story"), which I thought was fine.

## Ratings

- **Usefulness: 3/5**
- **Clarity: 2/5**
- **Trust: 3/5**

Clarity is lowest because I hit a word I didn't know, or an example about laptops and apps, on nearly every page, and each one made me feel the book was meant for someone cleverer than me.

## One change

Go through the whole book once with someone like me in mind, and every time a clinical word, tech word, business word or set of initials appears, explain it in plain words right there or swap it for an everyday one. That includes the examples: swap the app, the landing page, the framework and "ship" for the post, the dishes, the shift, the phone call you keep putting off. The tools are simple enough for me. The words wrapped around them aren't.
